Networks · Organizations · Digital platforms

I study how communication shapes social networks—and how those networks shape people's opportunities.

I am a master's student in Data Science at New York University. My research interests lie at the intersection of organizational research, social networks, and digital platforms.

I am particularly interested in how individuals build and use brokerage positions, how cultural alignment shapes their relationships within organizations, and how online communities organize around shared information and attention.

I use large-scale communication and behavioral data, network analysis, and natural language processing to study these questions.

Selected research

Culture and brokerage in organizations

I study how employees' cultural alignment and positions in communication networks develop over time. Using corporate email data, this work examines how fitting in and connecting across groups relate to cooperation and career outcomes, and how we can measure cultural fit from language.

With Bruno Abrahao and Christopher Rider

Communities and information on StockTwits

I helped construct and analyze an archive of more than 550 million StockTwits posts from approximately 7 million users, spanning 2008–2022. My work examines participation, sentiment, shared attention, and community structure in financial social media.

With Nasser Alansari and Aaron R. Kaufman

Measuring service quality through language

I develop NLP measures of service quality from large-scale Yelp review text. This collaborative project combines LLM-assisted labeling and text classification to study different dimensions of service quality and their relationships with economic outcomes.

With Bruno Abrahao, Olalekan Bello, Anton Bobrov, and James Traina
